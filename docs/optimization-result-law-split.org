* The principle

An estimator is two things wearing one coat: an *optimization* (find the
argmin of a criterion, and characterise the objective near it) and an
*inference* (turn that local characterisation into a statement about the
sampling distribution of the estimator). The second step is not free — it
requires *assumptions* (a CLT and that one is in the limit; or a resampling
DGP; ...). The current =EstimationResult= conflates them: it reports
=standard_errors=, =coef_table= (t / p), =J_pvalue=, and delta-method SEs —
all of which are *interpretations under an assumption* — right next to the
argmin.

The split:

- =OptimizationResult= — the solution to an optimization problem, and *only*
  objects intrinsic to it: the argmin, the objective value there, the
  gradient and the (Gauss--Newton) Hessian / Jacobian, the optimiser's trace,
  and the numerical conditioning. *No statistical interpretation.* It does not
  know the word "distribution".
- =EstimatorLaw= — consumes an =OptimizationResult= and is *explicit about the
  assumption it adds* to license a distributional reading. =AsymptoticLaw(result)=
  is the strong-assumption grade (a CLT, exact limiting behaviour); its very
  strength is what makes its queries simple. Weaker, more explicit grades
  (bootstrap / repeated-sampling =EmpiricalLaw=) carry more machinery.

*Membership rule.* A member lives on =OptimizationResult= iff computing it
needs only the data, the criterion, and its local geometry — no sampling
assumption. Everything else is an =EstimatorLaw= method that names its
assumption.

* =OptimizationResult= — the optimization surface

A plain, *directly constructible* =@dataclasses.dataclass= (host-side; not a
traced pytree — that role is =FitRecord='s). Directly constructible is a
first-class requirement (owner, 2026-07-03): a test must be able to hand-build
an *artificial* result (a chosen =gn_hessian= / =weighting_matrix= /
=moment_jacobian=) and feed it to =AsymptoticLaw= to unit-test the inference
assembly in isolation. So: sensible field order, no required
compute-coupled objects beyond the matrices, a =replace=-friendly shape.

| field              | meaning                                             | was            |
|--------------------+-----------------------------------------------------+----------------|
| =theta_hat=        | the minimiser (user parameter pytree)               | keep           |
| =objective_value=  | Q(θ̂) = m̄'Λm̄ at the optimum                       | =J_stat=       |
| =gradient=         | ∇Q(θ̂) — FOC residual at convergence                | /new/          |
| =moment_jacobian=  | G = ∂m̄/∂θ at θ̂ (manifold =G_riem=)                | internal only  |
| =gn_hessian=       | G'ΛG — Gauss--Newton curvature of the criterion     | =info_matrix=  |
| =weighting_matrix= | Λ — the realised, frozen, ridged metric in m̄'Λm̄   | =V_X= (see R4) |
| =n_overid=         | M − p_id — # overidentifying restrictions           | =J_dof=        |
| =optimizer_info=   | iterations / convergence / step                     | keep           |
| =diagnostics=      | Hessian conditioning κ, ridge τ (optimization only) | keep, trimmed  |
| =manifold_spec=    | leaf geometry                                       | keep           |
| =measure=,=covariance= | the criterion's definition (Law reads them)     | keep           |

*Removed from the result* (now =AsymptoticLaw= methods): =Sigma_theta=,
=standard_errors=, =coef_table=, =J_pvalue=, =J_pvalue_adjusted=,
=functional_se=, =eigenvalue_se=, =gamma_se=, =gamma_covariance=, =record()=.

=record()= moves to the studies layer as a free function
=emu_gmm.studies.fit_record(result)= (it is the empirical-law bridge and is
inherently statistical — it extracts an SE per rep). =OptimizationResult= keeps
*zero* statistical methods.

* =AsymptoticLaw= — the inference surface, assembled from ingredients

=AsymptoticLaw(result)= (the =EstimatorLaw= constructor the owner asked for).
It assembles the *ridge-correct sandwich* from the result's ingredients, so the
inference logic lives here, not in =estimate=:

- bread  B = =gn_hessian= = G'ΛG                    (from =moment_jacobian=, =weighting_matrix=)
- meat   M = G'Λ Ŝ ΛG,  Ŝ = raw =covariance.covariance(measure, θ̂)= (unridged; #138 meat)
- =cov()= = B⁻¹ M B⁻¹  (gauge-aware =pinv_eigvalrule= bread; collapses to B⁻¹ at
  efficient weighting + non-binding ridge)
- =se()=, =coef_table=, =functional_se= / leaf SEs, =j_test()= / =J_pvalue= +
  =J_pvalue_adjusted= (the χ² / Welch--Satterthwaite reading of =objective_value=),
  and it owns the #138 indefinite-meat NaN-SE warning.

** Why the Hessian alone is not enough (the ridge)

With a binding ridge (or any inefficient weighting) Λ = (V+τD)⁻¹ ≠ Ŝ⁻¹, so
M ≠ B and Σ is a genuine sandwich B⁻¹ M B⁻¹ — not B⁻¹ (#133). The Law therefore
needs (i) the *realised, frozen* =weighting_matrix= Λ for the bread (anchor-once:
a re-derived ridge could pick a different τ), and (ii) the raw meat Ŝ = V(θ̂),
which it recomputes from =result.covariance= + =result.measure= + θ̂. Both are on
the result, so no extra data threads through; the sandwich shortcut is only
valid at efficient weighting with a non-binding ridge.

* The shared routine

The sandwich (whiten, =pinv_eigvalrule= bread, gauge-nullspace handling, #138
meat detection) is extracted *byte-for-byte* into a pure low-level function
=emu_gmm._internal.asymptotic.asymptotic_covariance(G, weighting, meat, manifold_spec)
-> Σ= that =types.py= can import without a cycle. Both =AsymptoticLaw.cov()= and
the studies =fit_record= call it — one implementation, no =types → law= import.

* Construction & access

- =estimate()= returns the leaner =OptimizationResult=.
- =AsymptoticLaw(result)= is the inference constructor; =result.asymptotic()= is a
  convenience alias.
- Artificial results: =OptimizationResult(theta_hat=..., moment_jacobian=G,
  weighting_matrix=Lambda, ...)= builds directly (defaults for optimizer_info /
  diagnostics), so =AsymptoticLaw(OptimizationResult(...))= is unit-testable with
  known linear algebra.

* Migration

Internal consumers repoint by the membership rule:
- curvature users → the result (=identification_strength= builds G'ΛG Schur
  complements: reads =gn_hessian= directly, needs no V*);
- distribution users → the Law (=k_statistic= V-override, =cluster_bootstrap=,
  =wild_bootstrap=, =j_test=, =functional_se=, =persistence=, =studies=).
=EstimationResult= → =OptimizationResult= rename across src + tests. Docs:
=design.org= §"downstream contract" + =CLAUDE.md= repointed — SEs / p-values come
from =result.asymptotic()=, not =result=.
