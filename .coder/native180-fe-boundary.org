#+TITLE: Native product-effect reduction and amplitude boundary -- issue 180
#+DATE: [2026-10-29 Thu]

* Result and scope

This continues [[file:native180-limit-identification.org][the conditional native two-way limit]] without a new fit or a changed inference rule.
Two gaps can be sharpened analytically: consistent estimated product effects suffice to preserve the scaled two-way covariance under explicit cluster-energy bounds, and the fixed-ray CU criterion has a finite amplitude-zero limit whenever its limiting regularized covariance is positive definite.
Neither result proves that the native restricted problem attains an interior minimum.
The seven original grid points remain unknown.
Ledger Section 37 records the scope and source anchors.

* Estimated product effects in the two-way covariance

Use the full-mask, unit-weight native setting, fixed product count \(P\), \(N/(TH)\to\mu>0\), and \(r^2=(T^{-1}+H^{-1})^{-1}\).
Let \(B_{th}\in\mathbb R^{M\times P}\) have column \(p\) equal to the instrument total for product \(p\) in intersection cell \((t,h)\).
Assume the instruments sum to zero within each intersection cell and over all rows of each product, as in the exact-centering idealization of the prior note.
If \(T^0_{th}\) uses a deterministic population product coefficient \(a^0\), replacing it by the fitted coefficient gives the exact identity
\[
T_{th}-T^0_{th}=-B_{th}e,\qquad e=\widehat a-a^0.
\]
Cell effects disappear from this identity because the cell instrument total is zero.
Also \(\sum_{th}B_{th}=0\), so the aggregate moment is unchanged by this replacement, even when \(e\) depends on every observation.

Let \(\mathcal G\) contain all quarter groups, all recurring-market groups, and all intersection cells.
For any cell array, subscript \(g\) denotes summation over that group; give quarter and market groups sign \(+1\), and intersections sign \(-1\).
Define unsigned energies
\[
\mathcal E_X=\frac{r^2}{N^2}\sum_{g\in\mathcal G}\|X_g\|_F^2,
\qquad
\mathcal E_B=\frac{r^2}{N^2}\sum_{g\in\mathcal G}\|B_g\|_F^2.
\]
Suppose \(\mathcal E_{T^0}=O_p(1)\), \(\mathcal E_B=O_p(1)\), and \(\|e\|=o_p(1)\).
Writing \(\Delta_g=-B_ge\), one has \(\mathcal E_\Delta\leq\|e\|^2\mathcal E_B=o_p(1)\).
The triangle inequality and Cauchy--Schwarz give
\[
\left\|\frac{r^2}{N^2}\sum_g\operatorname{sign}(g)
 (T_gT_g'-T_g^0T_g^{0\prime})\right\|_F
\leq 2\sqrt{\mathcal E_{T^0}\mathcal E_\Delta}+\mathcal E_\Delta=o_p(1).
\]
Thus the fitted-product and population-product two-way covariances have the same scaled limit.
The bound uses unsigned energies, so it remains valid for an indefinite signed assembly and requires no independence between \(e\), \(B\), and \(T^0\).

For parameter derivatives in a fixed local quotient chart, the data and hence \(B\) do not depend on the parameter.
Consequently \(D_g-D_g^0=-B_g E\), where \(E=\partial\widehat a/\partial\theta'-\partial a^0/\partial\theta'\), and derivative arrays may be vectorized for the Frobenius norm.
If \(\mathcal E_{D^0}=O_p(1)\) and \(\|E\|_F=o_p(1)\), the corresponding derivative energy tends to zero.
Expanding each derivative--moment product bounds its scaled difference by
\[
\sqrt{\mathcal E_{\Delta D}\mathcal E_{T^0}}
+\sqrt{\mathcal E_{D^0}\mathcal E_\Delta}
+\sqrt{\mathcal E_{\Delta D}\mathcal E_\Delta}=o_p(1).
\]
This is exactly the replacement needed for the matched signed K cross covariance; it does not establish its population limit or weak-interest process independence.
Uniform versions of these assumptions give the same conclusion at a random consistent restricted fit.
Pointwise bounds alone do not justify substitution of an estimated parameter.

The energy assumptions are compatible with ordinary bounded-size cells: \(\sum_{th}\|B_{th}\|_F^2=O_p(TH)\) implies, by summing within each margin,
\[
\sum_t\|B_t\|_F^2\leq H\sum_{th}\|B_{th}\|_F^2,
\qquad
\sum_h\|B_h\|_F^2\leq T\sum_{th}\|B_{th}\|_F^2.
\]
Since \(r^2=TH/(T+H)\), these inequalities imply \(\mathcal E_B=O_p(1)\); the same argument applies to \(T^0\) and \(D^0\) under their cell second-moment bounds.
Only consistency of the product coefficients and derivatives is needed here, rather than a cell-count square-root rate.
Approximate instrument centering requires additional bounds on its aggregate and cell-effect errors; small group means on one frozen panel do not establish such rates.

* A primitive route to the auxiliary coefficient rate

For cell \(c=(t,h)\), let \(n_c\) be its \(P\)-vector of product counts, \(n_{c+}>0\) its row count, \(y_c(\theta)\) its vector of product outcome sums, and \(y_{c+}(\theta)\) its total.
The existing Schur reducer has
\[
S=\sum_c S_c,\quad S_c=\operatorname{diag}(n_c)-n_cn_c'/n_{c+},
\qquad
h(\theta)=\sum_c h_c(\theta),\quad
h_c=y_c-n_c y_{c+}/n_{c+}.
\]
With a fixed orthonormal product-contrast basis \(Q\),
\[
\widehat a(\theta)=Q(Q'SQ)^{-1}Q'h(\theta).
\]
Empty cells can instead contribute zero to both sums, provided that convention is specified before taking expectations.

A sufficient primitive construction is that each cell's count and outcome data are the same measurable function of \((U_t,V_h,E_{th})\), where the three families are mutually independent and iid within each family, with fixed laws and a fixed cell function as the margins grow.
Suppose the entries of \(S_c\), \(h_c(\theta)\), and the required outcome derivatives have finite second moments, and \(Q'\mathbb E[S_c]Q\succ0\).
Cells sharing neither index are then independent.
Counting the possibly dependent cell pairs shows that the variance of each cell average is \(O(T^{-1}+H^{-1})\).
Chebyshev's inequality and matrix inversion on the fixed contrast space yield, pointwise,
\[
\widehat a(\theta)-a^0(\theta)=O_p(r^{-1}),\qquad
a^0(\theta)=Q(Q'\mathbb E[S_c]Q)^{-1}Q'\mathbb E[h_c(\theta)].
\]
The same argument gives this rate for a fixed number of outcome derivatives, since the count matrix is parameter-independent.
On a compact interior parameter neighborhood, integrable Lipschitz envelopes for these cell functions give uniform consistency by a finite-net LLN argument.
Uniform second-moment envelopes for the relevant \(B,T^0,D^0\) cells then supply the uniform energy bounds above.
These are sufficient assumptions on a specified array; sample connectedness does not replace the population Schur bound.

This auxiliary argument does not derive a native CLT for instrument-weighted residuals.
In particular, globally preprojected covariates or instruments are not automatically functions of just \((U_t,V_h,E_{th})\); their own projection replacement must be justified before applying a cell-array theorem.
[[https://www.cemmap.ac.uk/wp-content/legacy/uploads/main-1.pdf][Davezies, D'Haultfoeuille and Guyonvarch (2018), Sections 2--4]] provide empirical-process and variance results under separately exchangeable, dissociated arrays, with a nondegenerate limiting covariance for inference.
Those assumptions require a declared sampling model; they do not follow from the names of the two cluster keys.
The native quarter sequence may require a time-dependence model beyond this sufficient construction.

* Exact CU behavior on a fixed amplitude ray

Fix \(s,A,\pi\), set \(\delta=e^{-c_0}>0\), and retain the original panel and the original ridge anchor.
The implemented auxiliary projection is linear in the outcome.
Therefore the row contribution is exactly \(\psi_i(\delta)=\delta u_i-v_i\), where
\[
u_i=Z_i[M_F(e^{-sz}Y)]_i,\qquad v_i=Z_iq_i(A,\pi).
\]
This identity uses the implemented projection of the outcome and does not require exact instrument centering.
For any fixed collection of uncentered clustered summands with fixed signs and dof factors, including pair-only and quarter-plus-market-minus-pair,
\[
m(\delta)=\delta b-d,\qquad
V(\delta)=\delta^2V_{uu}-\delta(V_{uv}+V_{vu})+V_{vv}.
\]
Here \(b=N^{-1}\sum_i u_i\), \(d=N^{-1}\sum_i v_i\), and each covariance coefficient is the corresponding signed cluster product with the package's \(N^{-2}\) normalization.
No sample-size multiplier is added to the criterion.

For native =DiagonalTikhonov=, both tau and the diagonal/additive mode are frozen at the original warm anchor.
Its application is a fixed *linear map on the current covariance*,
\[
\mathcal L(V)=V+\tau\operatorname{diag}(V)
\quad\hbox{or}\quad
\mathcal L(V)=V+\tau\frac{\operatorname{tr}(V)}M I.
\]
The additive branch does not mean a parameter-independent \(\tau I\) ridge.
Thus \(V^\star(\delta)=\mathcal L(V(\delta))\) is still quadratic in amplitude.
This follows from =estimator.py:_apply_anchored= and =regularization.py:DiagonalTikhonov.apply_fixed_tau=; custom regularizers need a separate argument.

If \(C_0=\mathcal L(V_{vv})\succ0\), ordinary inverse continuity gives
\[
\lim_{\delta\downarrow0}Q(\delta)=d'C_0^{-1}d<\infty.
\]
If \(C_\infty=\mathcal L(V_{uu})\succ0\), then similarly
\[
\lim_{\delta\to\infty}Q(\delta)=b'C_\infty^{-1}b<\infty.
\]
Each positive-definite endpoint also ensures a neighborhood where that ray's covariance is invertible.
There is no assertion that it stays positive definite between the endpoints, especially for a signed assembly.
Whenever either endpoint condition holds, the criterion is not coercive along that escaping physical-\(c_0\) ray.
An interior minimum may nevertheless exist; finite limits alone locate neither the infimum nor a global minimizer, and fixing \(A,\pi\) is not profiling them.
Singular or indefinite endpoints require separate analysis rather than these formulas or an automatic ridge re-anchor.

On an interval with \(\Lambda(\delta)=V^\star(\delta)^{-1}\succ0\), direct differentiation gives
\[
Q'(\delta)=2b'\Lambda m
-m'\Lambda\frac{dV^\star}{d\delta}\Lambda m.
\]
The second term is the CU weight derivative and must be retained.
If the derivative at zero is positive, nearby positive amplitudes have a larger criterion than that fixed-ray endpoint; a negative derivative reverses that local comparison.
A zero derivative requires higher-order analysis.
None of these signs determines a reprofiled branch without controlling the other nuisance directions.

The physical half-criterion score and its GN diagonal satisfy
\[
g_{c_0}=-\frac\delta2 Q'(\delta),\qquad
H_{c_0c_0}=\delta^2 b'\Lambda(\delta)b.
\]
Hence a bounded nonzero amplitude derivative produces an arbitrarily small physical score as \(c_0\to\infty\), even though
\[
\frac{g_{c_0}^2}{H_{c_0c_0}}
=\frac{Q'(\delta)^2}{4b'\Lambda(\delta)b}
\longrightarrow\frac{Q'(0)^2}{4b'C_0^{-1}b},
\]
provided the denominator is positive.
On a full-rank nuisance quotient, Cauchy--Schwarz gives \(g'H^{-1}g\geq g_{c_0}^2/H_{c_0c_0}\).
A nonzero limit therefore prevents the resolved full FOC decrement from tending to zero merely through amplitude shrinkage, despite the vanishing unscaled physical score.
If retained curvature becomes numerically unresolved, the production guard supplies no certificate instead.
At exactly zero amplitude both the physical amplitude and interest moment columns vanish; this remains outside the interior inference contract.

* Verification and next empirical obligation

The inequalities above are deterministic conditional on their stated stochastic bounds; the Schur rate follows from the specified latent-array covariance count.
The amplitude formulas follow from linearity of the existing model's auxiliary projection, fixed signed cluster products, and the production frozen-mode regularizer.
An independent seed-180 NumPy algebra check on a 12-by-11 synthetic cell array verified both perturbation inequalities, the affine/quadratic identities, both endpoint limits, and the physical-score/GN ratio.
The covariance perturbation norm was 0.013672 against its 0.084286 bound; the derivative--moment perturbation norm was 0.012159 against its 0.133831 bound.
Across identity regularization and the two frozen-mode ridge maps, relative covariance-polynomial discrepancies were below \(3.2\times10^{-16}\), and central-difference discrepancies for the CU amplitude derivative were below \(3.6\times10^{-10}\).
These deterministic toy-array checks verify algebra only; they are neither a native package fit nor a sampling or coverage study.
No native statistic has been recomputed by an alternate implementation.
No optimizer, confidence law, historical artifact, acceptance tolerance, or package runtime code changes in this continuation.

The next empirical task is a separately declared finite-candidate and boundary comparison using package evaluations under the original warm anchor, with a retained genuine result for any new optimization.
It must distinguish a fixed ray from a reprofiled boundary branch and report covariance definiteness and resolved FOC availability.
The next theory task is to specify a native stochastic array that supplies the remaining moment/derivative process laws, population quotient-information bounds and interior branch separation.
The present lemmas reduce those obligations; they do not certify native coverage or close issue 180.
